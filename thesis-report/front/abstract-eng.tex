\begin{latin}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

This project designs, implements and evaluates a prototype Persian
speech-to-speech system that operates in full-duplex mode: the user microphone
stays open while the assistant reply is playing, and a classical detector
decision can stop the active browser audio source and carry the captured speech
forward as the next user turn. To bound execution risk, two tracks were pursued
in parallel: direct low-rank adaptation of the open-weight Moshika
speech-to-speech model to Persian, and a modular cascade combining NeMo Persian
speech recognition, an exact Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 text responder under a
frozen prompt, and Persian Piper speech synthesis.

A derived conversational corpus was constructed from an internal archive of
Persian spoken content, yielding 6,754 user/response pairs and 108.584 measured
hours of stereo training audio. Training, validation and test partitions were
isolated by source session identifier, and no group leakage was observed. An
interruption detector was implemented from energy, fundamental-frequency and
MFCC features with a gradient-boosted classifier.

After a predeclared seven-row eligibility stage passed, the Qwen3-4B prompt-v2
responder passed every one of seven predeclared automatic gates on a 40-row,
source-session-group-disjoint final test. All 240 expected judge calls were
valid, mean response relevance improved from 1.425 for the frozen
Qwen2.5-0.5B baseline to 3.150, mean coherence reached 3.325, and the paired
relevance win rate was 67.5\%. The judge was a same-family Qwen3.8-27B-FP8
model, so this metric is not a human or independent evaluation. The mechanical
cascade chain, with the Qwen2.5-0.5B responder, produced a Persian transcript,
a Persian reply and non-silent audio on 9 of 9 frozen validation rows with zero
rule-based fallbacks. An automatic re-recognition proxy over those same nine
outputs measured 7.14\% character error rate and 30.41\% word error rate. The
nine-row mechanics panel was not re-run after the responder was promoted to
Qwen3-4B.

The direct track, by contrast, reduced its teacher-forced objective across six
experimental generations but never produced a checkpoint satisfying the frozen
autoregressive runtime criterion. Generation v6.2 achieved a 32.20\% reduction
in in-sample text loss, yet all four of its checkpoints passed 0 of 9 runtime
rows. That track is therefore reported as a negative result with a positive
learning signal, and the cascade is treated as the delivered prototype. Browser
measurement on a physical 12--24 GB GPU, proof of a 500 ms end-to-end latency
bound, independently human-verified interruption labels, and a 5--10 participant
listening study were not completed within the project deadline and remain
explicit limitations.

\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:
Speech-to-speech, full-duplex dialogue, barge-in detection, Persian language,
large language models, cascaded systems, parameter-efficient adaptation

\end{latin}
